The scenario: You're about to advise Amara Chen, Microsoft's VP of Environmental Advancement, on how to reconcile the company's AI growth with its 2030 sustainability pledges. Microsoft committed to being carbon negative, water positive, and zero waste by 2030 — but emissions have risen 30% since that pledge.
Phase 1: Place Your Bet

Which strategy should Microsoft prioritize?

🏗️
Redesign the Machine
Modular Data Centers
Build next-gen modular data centers with PUE of 1.05 vs. conventional 1.57. Cuts capex 30-50% and enables liquid cooling that reduces water use by 90%.
⚡
Change the Fuel
Renewable Energy
Shift to 100% renewable power with solar at $27.53/MWh and wind at $37.46/MWh by 2028. Addresses the root cause: where the energy comes from, not just how much is used.
🔧
Tune the Engine
Optimize Existing
Server virtualization ($400K-$800K investment, 1-2 year payback) and liquid cooling retrofits saving 30-40% energy. Potential $10-30M savings per facility over 10 years.

Back it up with numbers from the case

Phase Guidance

What you're doing: Choosing which strategy Microsoft should prioritize and backing it with evidence from the case.
Why it matters: Microsoft can't do everything at once. With $5B in capital over five years, every dollar spent on modular data centers is a dollar NOT spent on renewables or optimization. Your job is to make the strongest case for one primary bet.
The tension: Optimization has the best short-term ROI ($400K-$800K investment, 1-2 year payback). But Microsoft's emissions rose 30% during a period of optimization. Is efficiency enough, or does the system need redesigning?

Phase Guidance

What you're doing: Your team is drafting Microsoft's moonshot — one audacious, specific, measurable goal with a deadline.
The Kennedy standard: Kennedy didn't say "improve space capabilities." He said "land a man on the moon and return him safely to earth, before this decade is out." Your goal needs that same specificity.
Requirements: Your moonshot must (1) include a measurable number, (2) address community impact — the people in Mount Pleasant, WI who'd lose agricultural land, the residents of Goodyear, AZ competing with a data center for water during drought, the farmers in Mekaguda, India whose water supply is threatened, and (3) identify a funding source using case numbers.
Leverage level: Remember Fischer & Riechers from Session 16. Parameters = adjusting numbers (efficiency). Design = changing rules and incentives. Intent = changing what the system is FOR. Which level is your moonshot operating at? Be honest — the class will see.

Phase Guidance

What you're doing: You have 100 budget points to distribute across all team proposals. This is your investment thesis — which moonshot would you actually fund?
Decision criteria to consider:
  • Does the moonshot interrupt the reinforcing loop (AI demand → more data centers → more AI) or just improve the balancing loop (efficiency gains)?
  • Is the target specific and measurable, or vague?
  • Is the funding mechanism realistic given the case numbers?
  • Does it address community impact, or just carbon?
  • What leverage level is it operating at — and is that deep enough?
Rule: You can allocate a maximum of 25 points to your own team's proposal. This forces you to genuinely evaluate others.
The ExxonMobil question: As you evaluate, consider: Microsoft also sells AI to fossil fuel companies to help them find MORE oil reserves. Does any proposal address this structural contradiction?
